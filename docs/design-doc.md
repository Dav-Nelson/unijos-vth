## Problem Statement

The University of Jos Veterinary Teaching Hospital (Unijos VTH) in Nigeria currently
operates on paper-based records. A former Unijos graduate is building a free, open-source
clinic management system to digitize three core workflows: reception (patient + owner
registration, appointments), clinical work (vet/student notes, diagnoses, prescriptions),
and billing (invoices, receipts, payment tracking).

Constraints: zero budget, solo developer (junior-to-mid level), Nigerian infrastructure
(low-bandwidth, unreliable power, low-end hardware), must run on Unijos ICT servers or
free cloud tiers. Must support teaching hospital workflows — student drafts that require
supervising vet sign-off before clinical notes are finalized.

Real stakeholder contact at the hospital has agreed to collaborate.

---

## What Makes This Different From a Normal Vet System

This is a **teaching hospital**. That distinction is load-bearing in the data model.
Not just a feature — it changes the schema from day one:

- Every clinical record has a status lifecycle: DRAFT → PENDING_REVIEW → APPROVED
- Every student action requires a supervisor's sign-off before it becomes part of the
  official record
- Cases must be exportable in anonymized form for teaching, grand rounds, and student
  presentations
- Student competency tracking (what procedures each student has performed)
- Supervising vet sees a queue of "awaiting my approval" clinical notes

A regular vet clinic doesn't have any of this.

---

## Premises Agreed

1. The hospital is currently paper-based. No existing digital migration baggage.
2. Reliable internet CANNOT be assumed mid-consultation. Clinical notes must not be
   lost if connectivity drops mid-examination.
3. The teaching workflow (draft → approval) is a data model constraint from day one,
   not a UI feature to layer on later.
4. You are the sole developer. Maintainability by Unijos ICT is a real concern.
   ICT's tech skills are currently unknown — this must be investigated before going
   further. It is a risk.
5. An MVP covering ONE workflow and trusted by real staff beats three half-finished
   workflows that nobody relies on.
6. Nigeria's NDPR (Data Protection Regulation) applies — owner personal data is stored.

## What You Might Be Missing (Critical Gaps)

These are things not in your original plan that will bite you:

### 1. Who maintains this after you?
**This is the #1 killer of volunteer hospital tech projects.**
You need a succession plan before you write line one. Options:
- Document everything so exhaustively that an Unijos ICT staff member can run it
- Teach a junior student at Unijos to maintain it as part of their coursework
- Plan for self-hosted Supabase so ICT controls the database without needing cloud accounts
- Write a MAINTAINERS.md from day one. Keep it honest.

### 2. Power cuts (NEPA)
Frequent power outages in Nigeria mean sessions get interrupted. Your clinical notes
form must auto-save to localStorage every 30 seconds. If the browser closes mid-exam,
reopening should restore the draft. This is not a nice-to-have — it's patient safety.

### 3. Printer compatibility
Receipt and patient card printing must work on cheap Epson/Canon inkjet printers and
thermal printers common in Nigerian hospitals. Use CSS @media print stylesheets.
Never rely on the browser's print dialog showing the right layout — test it on actual
cheap printers if possible.

### 4. Migration from paper
The hospital sees patients today. Those records exist on paper. You need a cutover
strategy: on what date does the digital system become the record of truth? Old paper
records don't need to be digitized, but new patients registered from Day 1 need a
clear process. Brief stakeholders on this explicitly.

### 5. The medication/drug list
Your prescriptions module will need a list of medications. Nigerian drug availability
differs from Western drug databases. Brand names differ from generics. Where does
this list come from? Who maintains it? Ask the vets — they likely have a standard
formulary they use.

### 6. Case anonymization for teaching
Grand rounds, clinical conferences, final year presentations — cases shared for
teaching must have owner info automatically stripped. Build an "Export for Teaching"
function that generates an anonymized PDF. This is what makes the portfolio story
interesting: "I solved a problem no commercial software handles cleanly for African
teaching hospitals."

### 7. NDPR compliance
Nigeria's Data Protection Regulation (similar to GDPR) covers owner personal data.
Minimum requirements:
- Data processed only for stated purposes
- Users can request data deletion
- Breach notification procedures documented
- If hosting on cloud, data residency may matter (Supabase has regions)
Add a basic privacy policy and data handling notice even for v1.

**NDPR v1 minimum scope (defer full compliance to Phase C):**
- Privacy notice shown at registration: "Your data is used to manage your animal's care."
- Admin can export or delete an owner's data on request (manual process is fine for v1)
- Breach notification procedure documented (even if it's just "email the hospital admin")
- Full requirements (data portability, right to object, DPIA) are deferred to Phase C
  when the system has real users and a clearer data processing picture.

### 8. Session persistence
If a vet closes the browser mid-consultation, do they lose their work? Answer must
be no. Auto-save drafts to the database (or localStorage as fallback) constantly.

### 9. The bus factor
You are the single point of failure. If you stop working on this tomorrow, what
happens? Write the README and setup guide as if explaining to a stranger. Use
conventional commits so git log tells the story. Document architectural decisions
in ADR (Architecture Decision Records) files.

### 10. Student assessment tracking
Teaching hospitals track student competency. Each procedure a student performs should
optionally be logged against a competency matrix (e.g., "performed physical examination
unsupervised"). Ask the clinical staff whether this is needed — it might be out of scope
for v1, but if it is needed, it affects the schema.

## Tech Stack Recommendation

### The Stack: Next.js 14 + Supabase + Prisma + shadcn/ui

**Why this, not something else:**

```
Frontend:     Next.js 14 (App Router)
              - SSR means fast first load on slow connections
              - React is the most hireable skill in 2026
              - Deploys to Vercel free tier with zero config
              - API Routes = no separate backend service to manage

Database:     PostgreSQL via Supabase
              - Free tier: 500MB DB, 1GB file storage (animal photos)
              - Built-in auth, row-level security
              - Can be SELF-HOSTED on Unijos ICT servers (Supabase is open source)
              - Daily backups on free tier

ORM:          Prisma
              - Type-safe database queries
              - Schema migrations tracked in git
              - Excellent portfolio signal — shows you understand data modeling

UI:           shadcn/ui + Tailwind CSS
              - Accessible components, works on all screen sizes
              - Tree-shakeable — small bundle = faster on slow connections
              - Looks professional without custom design work

Forms:        React Hook Form + Zod
              - Client-side validation before hitting the server
              - Zod schemas can be shared between frontend and backend

Auth:         Supabase Auth
              - Role-based access control (RBAC) built in
              - Row-level security lets you enforce "students only see their cases"
              at the database level — not just in the UI

Print:        CSS @media print + react-to-print
              - Patient cards, receipts, anonymized case exports
              - Works on any printer

State:        TanStack Query (React Query)
              - Smart caching for slow connections
              - Optimistic updates

Offline:      Auto-save form state to localStorage every 30s (recovery on refresh)
              Service worker caches app shell (loads even without network)
              Scope: recovery from browser crash / power cut, NOT offline editing.
              "Offline editing with sync when back online" = full offline mode = out of scope.
              User communication: "Your note is saved. If power cuts, just refresh."

Deployment:   Phase A: Vercel (free tier) + Supabase cloud. No ICT dependency.
              Phase C+: Evaluate Docker Compose on ICT server AFTER confirming ICT
              can run Docker + PostgreSQL. Do not assume this works until confirmed.
```

### Why NOT a separate backend (Express/FastAPI)?
You're solo. Two services to deploy, debug, and keep in sync doubles your operational
surface area. Next.js API Routes cover everything you need for this scale.

### Why NOT Laravel/PHP?
Better for maintainability by ICT if they know PHP. BUT: less portfolio value for
remote jobs, slower UI development, and the Unijos ICT skills are unknown anyway.
Investigate ICT's skills before finalizing. If they're PHP-only, reconsider.

### Why NOT a mobile app?
Web app works on phones too (responsive). A mobile app doubles your workload and
requires app store accounts (costs money) or sideloading (security risk). Web first.

## Hosting: Free Cloud vs Unijos ICT Servers

**Recommendation: Start on free cloud tiers. Plan for ICT migration.**

### Free Cloud (Vercel + Supabase)
Pros:
- Deploy in minutes with zero IT department dependency
- More reliable than an unknown university server
- Automatic daily database backups
- HTTPS by default
- Can iterate without waiting for IT approval

Cons:
- Owner PII leaves Nigeria (NDPR consideration — choose Supabase West EU region as closest
  available option for Phase A. Long-term, migration to Unijos ICT resolves this completely.
  The two strategies don't contradict: use cloud for Phase A, migrate to ICT when ready.)
- Free tiers can change (Supabase has changed pricing before)
- Vercel free: 100GB bandwidth/month (fine for this scale)

### Unijos ICT Servers (Preferred long-term)
Pros:
- Data stays within university network and country
- No vendor lock-in
- No cost concerns long-term
- Faster for users on campus network

Cons:
- Unknown server specs, OS, reliability
- May not have Node.js, Docker, or PostgreSQL
- IT departments in Nigerian universities move slowly
- No guaranteed uptime SLA
- Backup practices unknown

### The Questions to Ask ICT Before Deciding
Ask these BEFORE committing to hosting there:
1. What OS do the servers run? (Linux? Windows Server?)
2. Can they run Docker containers?
3. Do they have PostgreSQL available?
4. What is their backup policy? How often? Where are backups stored?
5. What is their typical uptime / do they have planned downtime windows?
6. Who has access to the server if you're unavailable?

If the answers are concerning: stay on Vercel + Supabase. The Docker Compose setup
means you can migrate when ICT is ready. Don't let perfect be the enemy of shipped.

## Teaching Hospital Specific Features

These are what make this system different from any vet clinic tutorial project:

### 1. The Approval Chain (Core Differentiator)
```
Student creates clinical note (DRAFT)
  → Saves it, notifies supervising vet
  → Supervising vet sees queue: "2 notes awaiting review"
  → Vet reviews, requests changes OR approves
  → APPROVED note becomes the official medical record
  → Approval record is immutable: { vetId, timestamp, approvalHash }
```
Legally, a student's clinical decisions must be validated by a licensed vet before
being actioned. This is not optional — it's patient safety.

**What "signing off" means technically:**
When a supervising vet clicks "Approve", the system records in the database:
- `approved_by_vet_id` (foreign key to staff table)
- `approved_at` (UTC timestamp)
- `approval_note` (optional text vet's comment)
- `record_hash` (SHA-256 of the clinical note content at approval time)

The record_hash creates an audit trail: if anyone edits the note post-approval,
the hash no longer matches. This is NOT a cryptographic signature (too complex
for v1) but it's an immutable audit log. The approved note is read-only after
approval — only admin can amend, and amendments create a new version, not
overwrite the original.

### 2. Case Assignment
Not every student sees every case. Students are assigned to cases (rotations).

**Visibility rules:**
- A student sees ONLY cases they are explicitly assigned to
- A supervising vet sees ALL cases, with a filtered view of "assigned to me"
- Admin and receptionist see all cases (read-only clinical data)
- Assignment persists in history — even after a rotation ends, the student
  can view (read-only) cases they were assigned to for their competency record

**Who assigns:**
- Admin or supervising vet assigns students to cases at or after registration
- This is a deliberate action — not automatic on case creation
- UI: On any case page, vet/admin sees "Assign Student" dropdown

**Permission boundary:**
Row-level security in Supabase enforces this at the database level.
A student cannot query a case they're not assigned to — not just hidden in UI,
actually blocked in the database query. This prevents accidental data exposure.

### 3. Anonymized Case Export
"Export for Teaching" button on any approved case:
- Strips owner name, phone, address
- Replaces with "Owner A" or random ID
- Keeps all clinical data, diagnosis, treatment
- Exports as PDF
- This is what gets shown at grand rounds, conferences, student presentations

### 4. Supervising Vet's Queue
A dedicated view: "Cases Awaiting My Approval" — sorted by time waiting, with
one-click review and approve/reject with comments. This is the clinical equivalent
of a PR review queue. Vets are busy; make this as fast as possible.

### 5. Student Rotation Log (OPEN QUESTION — confirm with hospital before building)
Each student's history of case assignments is automatically logged as a side effect
of the case assignment system. Whether to build a formal competency matrix (procedures
performed, supervisor ratings) is an open question — see Open Questions section.
Do not build competency tracking until the hospital confirms they want it.

### 6. Case Tagging for Research
Vets may want to tag cases for potential research use. Simple boolean flag: "flag
for research follow-up." Keeps scope minimal but enables future analytics.

### 7. Clinical Conference / Grand Rounds Mode
A read-only presentation view of a case — clean, large text, no edit buttons —
that can be pulled up on a projector during clinical conference. No PDF needed;
just a clean URL the vet can navigate to on any screen.

## Stakeholder Communication Plan

### The Golden Rule
**Show, don't tell. Never send a text description of a feature. Always send something
they can see or click.**

### Tools
- **WhatsApp groups** — the default for Nigerian professional communication.
  Use it for quick updates, sharing screen recording links, informal feedback.
- **Figma** (free tier) — for interactive prototypes before building.
  Share a link they can open on their phone and click through.
- **Loom** (free tier, 5 min) — for screen recordings showing how a workflow works.
  Much easier for non-technical people than screenshots.
- **Google Forms** — for structured feedback after showing mockups.
  Simple questions: "What did you like? What was confusing? What is missing?"
- **Google Slides** — for formal milestone presentations. Printable.

### Communication Rhythm

**Before building anything (now — allow 1–2 weeks for real feedback, not days):**
- Share Figma prototype of Phase A (reception workflow only)
- Google Form to collect preferences: "How do you want patient cards numbered?
  What information is most important to see at a glance?"
- WhatsApp: "Here's a clickable demo — try registering a fake patient. 
  Tell me what feels wrong."
- Reality check: non-technical stakeholders often reply "looks good!" to prototypes
  without real engagement. The real feedback comes when they use the live system.
  Don't wait forever for prototype feedback — set a deadline (1 week), then build.

**After Phase A ships:**
- Short Loom video: "Here's what I built. Here's how it works."
- WhatsApp: "Is anyone at the hospital who can test this with me on a call?"
- Observe someone using it in real time — Zoom screen share works fine.
  Don't help them. Watch where they get stuck.

**After Phase C ships:**
- Formal email with screenshots + summary of what changed.
- Walk through the approval workflow with a doctor and a student together.
  This is the critical feedback session.

**Monthly:**
- WhatsApp message: "Quick update — here's what I've done this month + 
  what's next. Any questions or feedback?"
- Keep it short. They're busy.

### What to Show Non-Technical People
- Always show the WORKFLOW, not the code. "Here is what a receptionist does 
  when a new patient arrives" — step by step, clickable.
- Use realistic fake data. Not "John Doe" — use Nigerian names, local addresses,
  real animal species common in Plateau State.
- Print a mockup and hand it to the receptionist. Paper is still powerful.
- Never show them a database schema. Never.
